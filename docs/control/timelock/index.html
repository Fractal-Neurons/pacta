Protocols behind a timelock, by length of delay

75 of the 324 protocols we have checked put a delay (a timelock) between a decision to change a contract and the change taking effect. Each protocol's delay is the shortest one read on its sampled timelocked controls; the list runs shortest delay first. The shortest delay is 1 hour; the longest is 10 days. 193 protocols are left out because the delay could not be determined, and 56 because at least one of their sampled controls has a delay of zero seconds, that is no delay at all; a delay of zero is not a timelock. What the delay means

A timelock holds a decided change for a fixed time before it can run. During that time anyone can see what is coming. A longer delay gives users more time to react; it says nothing about whether a change is good.

The call, for Stader Labs (ETHx) on Ethereum: GET https://api.pacta.wtf/v1/check?chain=ethereum&address=0xA35b1B31Ce002FBF2058D22F30f95D405200A15b, or the MCP tool who_controls with the chain and the address. 100 calls a day are free, no key.
