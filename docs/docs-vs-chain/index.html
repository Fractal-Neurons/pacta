Protocols whose documents differ from the chain

For 42 of the 189 protocols whose documents we compared with the chain, the documents and the chain disagree on how the contracts are controlled. Each row names what the documents say, what the chain shows, and the block it was read at. A difference can be an outdated document; the row states both sides and takes neither. Of the 324 protocols we have checked, the documents of 189 were compared with the chain and those of 135 were not; of the 189 compared, 42 differ and are listed here. Not listed: 126 where the two agree, 7 where the chain is stricter than documented, and 14 where a difference is not determined. The list runs by protocol name, A to Z; newest reading 2026-11-20. A protocol leaves the list when a newer reading of its record shows the two agree.

How a difference is found

Each record reads one control setting from the chain at a pinned block and records what the protocol's documents say about it. 35 of the 42 rows above quote the documents' own words; the other 7 give the record's summary of them, marked in the row. Where the two differ, the record marks the difference. Each compared finding is attested on Base. The attestation carries the block it was read at and a fingerprint (SHA-256) of the summary that holds the quoted text; the text itself is in the public summary, not onchain. The method page gives the rules.

How a protocol team can have a row corrected: Submit updated docs, the free queue for updated documents.

The call, for Stader Labs (ETHx) on Ethereum: GET https://api.pacta.wtf/v1/check?chain=ethereum&address=0xA35b1B31Ce002FBF2058D22F30f95D405200A15b, or the MCP tool who_controls with the chain and the address. 100 calls a day are free, no key.
